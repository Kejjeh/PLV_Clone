Key Findings

What the data actually says about hitting and pitching, mapped to your fantasy scoring

🏆 Blast% is the best single hitting proxy

r = +0.74
Blast% → xwOBA

Blast (hard hit + sweet spot angle) explains 55% of xwOBA variance. It's your best cheap-to-compute proxy for real contact quality — more explanatory than hard hit% alone (r=+0.69) or barrel% (r=+0.86, but noisier at small samples).

⚠️ Core FP/PA ≠ Power Proxy

r = +0.34
Blast% → Core FP/PA

Blast% surprisingly has only moderate correlation with core fantasy points. Why? Core FP/PA heavily weights K avoidance and BB, not just power. Whiff% is the single biggest drag (r=−0.63 with Core FP/PA).

🎯 The two-axis hitter model

Discipline × Power
Chase% + Whiff% + Blast%

The ideal fantasy hitter scores on both axes: low chase (→BB, avoids K), low whiff (→contact quality), high blast (→TB). Ohtani, Soto, and Judge all clear 14%+ blast and sub-14% whiff. That combination is extremely rare.

🔥 PLV is a near-perfect FP/IP predictor

r = +0.95
PLV → FP/IP

PLV explains 89% of FP/IP variance (r²=0.895) — essentially a complete picture. Whiff% adds info for K rate specifically. Called strike% is the best proxy for walk rate (r=−0.96 with BB/IP).

🛑 Chase% = BB% (exactly)

r = −1.00
Chase% → BB/PA

Chase rate is literally the same signal as walk rate in your model (r=−1.000). If you want to identify walk contributors, chase% is your number. Decision+ and BB% are near-identical rankings.

📊 Whiff% is the strikeout key

r = +0.90
Whiff% → K/PA

Whiff rate explains 81% of K rate variance. It's a better predictor than swing% or chase% for Ks. High whiff = K liability for hitters, K generator for pitchers.

Stat Tiers — Hitting

Ranked by explanatory power for your fantasy scoring

Tier 1 — Core signal (use these to rank)

Process+ (r=+0.61 → Core FP/PA) · Whiff% (r=−0.63) · Contact+ (r=+0.53) · Avg EV (r=+0.40). These are the direct inputs to your best-calibrated model components.

Tier 2 — Power proxy (good for TB/RBI upside)

Power+ (r=+0.90 → TB/PA) · Blast% (r=+0.71) · Barrel% (r=+0.71) · xwOBA (r=+0.87). These tell you about raw damage — but remember TB is only 1 part of your scoring.

Tier 3 — Contextual / noisy

Sweet spot% (r=+0.18 — weakest batted ball metric) · Decision+ (r=−0.26 with Core FP) · Full FP/PA (R/RBI contamination). Use directionally, not for ranking.

Stat Tiers — Pitching

Tier 1 — PLV dominates

PLV (r=+0.95 → FP/IP, r²=0.895). Nothing else comes close. If a pitcher has elite PLV (5.3+), everything else is a downstream consequence. Start here, always.

Tier 2 — Rate-specific predictors

CS% (r=−0.96 → BB/IP — this is basically the same metric). Whiff% (r=+0.64 → K/IP). If you want to find K upside beyond PLV, add whiff. If you want to find walk risk, use CS%.

Tier 3 — Noise

xwOBA model (r=+0.27 → ER/IP — barely explains anything). H/IP is BABIP-contaminated. ER/IP predictions have high variance. Don't over-index on projected ERA.

Key takeaways

PLV r=+0.95 with FP/IP — this is as clean as baseball stats get. R²=0.895.
CS% r=−0.96 with BB/IP — called strike rate is almost perfectly predictive of walks. A pitcher who can't get called strikes will walk batters.
PLV StdDev r=−0.59 with FP/IP — high variance pitchers (inconsistent PLV) are punished. Consistency matters, not just average quality.
xwOBA model is surprisingly weak (r=+0.27 for ER) — your PLV scores capture most pitcher value already; xwOBA adds marginal signal at best.
