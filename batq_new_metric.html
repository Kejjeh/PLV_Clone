A new composite designed specifically around your scoring rules (TB+1, BB+1, K−1, HBP+1). Built from four orthogonal axes, each z-scored within season, then optimally weighted against actual realized fantasy points.

Why these four axes?
Your scoring formula (TB + BB − K + HBP) decomposes into three near-independent dimensions of batter skill. Contact quality (xwOBA on contact) drives TB. K avoidance (1−Whiff%) drives the biggest penalty in your system — K is −1 pt and is the single highest-variance component. In-play frequency multiplies contact quality: even elite contact means nothing if the ball stays on the bat. Walk selectivity (1−Chase%) was the weakest axis in the data — BB/PA is surprisingly less predictive of total FP than K rate.

Three Score Levels

Fantasy batting performance breaks into three cleanly separable layers — each with a different data requirement and reliability level.

Level 1 — Pure Bat Control

FP_L1 = TB + BB − K + HBP

Most reliable. 100% derivable from pitch-level Statcast. No context dependence — this is what the batter controls at the plate, full stop. Stabilizes fastest (TB ~150 PA, K/BB ~50 PA). This is what BATQ and core_fp_per_pa are designed to predict.

Best predictors: Whiff% (K), xwOBA on contact + InPlay% (TB), Chase% (BB).

Level 2 — Bat Control + Speed

FP_L2 = L1 + SB

Moderately reliable. Adds stolen base value. SB is the hardest of the non-context stats to predict from Statcast because sprint speed and opportunity (not just ability) drive it. The model uses Bayesian shrinkage toward league average — best signal comes after 150+ PA.

Best predictor: observed SB rate (shrunk). Sprint speed would help but isn't in your current data.

Level 3 — Full Fantasy Score

FP_L3 = L2 + R + RBI

Context dependent. R and RBI are the noisiest components — they require lineup environment (who's batting around the player, team OBP, batting order position). A .380 OBP player batting 8th scores ~40% fewer fantasy runs than the same player batting 2nd. See the Environment tab.

Best predictors: OBP proxy (for R) + avg runners on base when batting (for RBI).

Honest Assessment — What We Actually Know

Building new metrics is easy. Knowing what they're actually worth is harder. Here's the unvarnished picture.

BATQ's cross-validation problem: In-sample r²=0.66 looks great. 5-fold CV r²=0.34 is much weaker — and the CV variance was huge (±0.30 std dev). This means the metric weights are somewhat overfit to 2023+2024 data. With only 815 player-seasons across 2 years, there's not enough data to fully stabilize a 4-weight fit. Until we validate against a third year (2025), treat BATQ as directionally correct but not reliably better than core_fp_per_pa for ranking purposes.
What is actually robust: The direction of the four axes is clearly correct and stable — xwOBA on contact, K avoidance, and in-play frequency all point the right way. The relative ordering at the extremes (Ohtani, Soto, Judge at top; replacement-level players at bottom) is reliable. The middle of the distribution is noisier.
What would make BATQ better:
1. More seasons — add 2021 and 2022 Statcast data to get to 4 years (~1,600 player-seasons). CV r² would likely improve to 0.50+.
2. Sprint speed — adding Statcast sprint speed (ft/sec) would significantly improve the SB component, currently the weakest axis.
3. Pull% / GB% — groundball rate limits TB even on well-struck contact. This is partially captured by in_play_pct but could be more explicit.
4. Year-over-year weight — a rolling 2-year weighted average of BATQ (70% current / 30% prior year) would reduce variance without much bias cost.

Practical Recommendation

For ranking purposes: core_fp_per_pa is still your best ranking metric — it has better CV stability and years of calibration. BATQ is most useful as a quick sanity check on players who are new to the model (early season, recent debuts) because it needs only raw Statcast inputs rather than the full model pipeline.

For understanding: the three-axis framing (contact quality × in-play frequency × K avoidance) is the right mental model. Any player you evaluate, run those three numbers in your head. A player who scores elite on all three is genuinely rare — that's your target.

For R/RBI: use environment as a +/− adjustment on top of L1 rankings, not as a primary driver. The effect (±6 pts/600 PA) is real but small compared to the bat-control signal (range of ~120 pts/600 PA between best and worst hitters). Don't reach for a bad hitter because he bats cleanup — the lineup context bump doesn't overcome a 0.05 lower core_fp_per_pa.
