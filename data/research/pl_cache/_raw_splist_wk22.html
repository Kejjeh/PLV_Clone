Top 100 Starting Pitchers For 2026 Fantasy Baseball 8/18: Week 22 Rankings

Nick's weekly Top 100 Starting Pitcher rankings update.

Nick Pollack
The List 4 Hours Ago

Have questions? My “office hours” are on Twitch 10:00 am – 12:00 pm ET Monday – Friday + the aforementioned stream of The List.

It’s time for regular updates to THE LIST, where I rank the best 100 starting pitchers in baseball every week.

For each edition of The List, I have a set of rules to outline my thought process and how to best use these rankings. Please take note:

  1. This is 5×5, 12-teamer, H2H format focused (Wins, not QS). It generally is the same as roto as well, but make sure you adjust accordingly.
  2. Before the notes and rankings, an injury table outlines where players would be relatively ranked if they were fully healthy. It’s the best way to tackle how to value players on the IL.
  3. If a player is on the IL or not confirmed inside the rotation, they aren’t on the List. That includes injuries and guys in the minors, but there are exceptions for players who are expected to be in the rotation but are being skipped this week.
  4. Since this is a 12-teamer, I heavily weigh upside in the back-half of the rankings. The HIPSTER players are likely going to underperform those labeled as a Toby across a full season, but it’s in your best interest to chase ceiling vs. floor in many cases. Pick the one that you need most in your situation, of course.
  5. I’ve made a decision to limit labels to just two labels per player, with few exceptions for a second. It streamlines the process much better and hopefully gives you a more targeted understanding of the player.
  6. The notes outline oh-so-much to help your team. Please read the notes if you can instead of just scrolling to the bottom.

Let’s get to it.

 

Ranking Notes

 

  • This is your reminder to please read these notes as they’ll tell you plenty about why “someone moved up” or “why is he at #X?!”
  • Seriously. Read the notes.
  • I HAVE MOVED IL ARMS OFF THE LIST. It’s the reason why a lot of arms have gone up.
  • I know there are going to be a ton of comments about I hate how much these rankings change each week and I’m going to get out ahead of them here.
  • These rankings mostly change in the back half of The List as that’s your waiver wire. Those aren’t the players you hold onto throughout the year like your SPs 1-4, which means we’re going to be a bit more chaotic and roll with the waves more aggressively. If I see elements that suggest a pitcher could be a Top 40 arm, I’m going to move up a ton from the 80s to the 60s. Shazam, there we go.

Tier 1 – It’s Nice To Not Worry

 

1. Jacob Misiorowski (MIL) – I’m kinda incredulous that it’s mid-August and he’s still doing it.

2. Tarik Skubal (LAD) – Skubal is 0-2 in three starts for the Dodgers thus far. That’s baseball, Suzyn.

3. Chris Sale (ATL) – The dude is still dope and makes us feel dope.

4. Cam Schlittler (NYY) – Despite having a worse fastball last time out, he’s still fantastic.

 

Tier 2 – The Other Aces 

 

5. Yoshinobu Yamamoto (LAD) – Good ole consistency, once a week over the weekend.

6. Drew Rasmussen (TBR) – Rasmussen was down in velocity and I’m not worried about it. One start.

7. Chase Burns (CIN) – I was concerned last week that something was wrong and he responded with a return to his former velocity. Nice.

8. Logan Gilbert (SEA) – Gilbert is still pounding heaters over the plate effectively, creating a stronger floor than seasons past.

9. Dylan Cease (TOR) – Cease’s walk rate will continue to keep the WHIP higher than we like, but that strikeout rate is bonkers.

10. Jesús Luzardo (PHI) – He’s the same Luzardo you’ve seen all year.

11. Gerrit Cole (NYY) – Nothing to fear about Cole, but I wish his slider and four-seamer teamed up to be phenomenal for a game.

12. Zack Wheeler (PHI) – Wheeler had lower velocity and is giving us a little bit of anxiety.

13. Gavin Williams (CLE) – It was a clear anomaly for Gavin with fastballs failing to find strikes. He’ll bounce back.

14. Jacob deGrom (TEX) – It’s hard not to worry about the biceps fatigue, and I didn’t feel right removing the AGA tag until we heard more information. We still don’t know if he’s starting this week.

 

Tier 3 – You’re Dope But Not Dope. Yet.

 

15. Logan Henderson (MIL) – His Coronation Day potentially arrives this week against Atlanta. Yes, I’d have him over deGrom if we had more clarity of Jacob’s injury. Same goes for all of this tier.

16. Blake Snell (LAD) – It’s #HotSnellSummer.

17. Payton Tolle (BOS) – Tolle has been a stud as of late, featuring more strikes with his Ras Pack than earlier in the year.

18. Eury Pérez (MIA) – Pérez is getting more done with his slider to RHB, and I’m happy the fastballs find success, even if they need a touch more polish.

19. Cristopher Sánchez (PHI) – Sánchez is a threat to your WHIP lately, fueled by a 50% strike changeup and a 94 mph fastball. Both marks should improve in time.

20. Paul Skenes (PIT) – Skenes’ start was delayed by the Pirates to later in the week and boy do I hope it allows him to gear up to 98 mph and toss more than 70 pitches. It’s not like he’s been bad, you know.

21. Troy Melton (DET) – Melton is rock solid, but there’s one final piece missing before he can return the strikeouts required of the AGA tag.

22. Cade Cavalli (WSH) – The upped velocity and reliance on the cutter have put Cavalli on another level.

 

Tier 4 – Far Sweeter Than Sour

 

23. Reid Detmers (LAA) – Detmers is still awesome and I’m just holding onto hope he keeps it rolling for the rest of the season.

24. Parker Messick (CLE) – It’s hard not to like the SWATCH approach at 95 mph as it continues to perform.

25. Sean Burke (CHW) – Burke takes a tumble after another 4.1 inning outing of disappointment. It’s a weird moment where it feels atypical, but it’s also the same extreme happening close to each other. If the four-seamer stays up and the curve + slider support it, we should be all good.

26. Sandy Alcantara (MIA) – He’s a QS machine. It’s as simple as that.

27. Nolan McLean (NYM) – I’m watching McLean and see a young arm still trying to figure out his entire arsenal and how to wrangle it to create a reliable approach.

28. Kyle Harrison (MIL) – Say what you want about the scoring change of his last start, he still pitched badly. All I want is for him to redeem himself tonight.

29. Braxton Ashcraft (PIT) – Ashcraft went the distance and efficiently for the first time, boasting an effective curveball to LHB. It’s about time we saw that again. Now fix the slider.

 

Tier 5 – Everyone Who Sees This Wants It Ranked Differently

 

30. Trevor Rogers (BAL) – It’s 94/95 mph up and changeups + breakers down. You can throw away all memories of the terrible four-inning game now.

31. Sonny Gray (BOS) – He was Sonny in lovely PNC Park and he could soar the rest of the way.

32. Carlos Rodón (NYY) – It’s a Still ILL for Rodón after tossing under 60 pitches in his last tune-up in Double-A. It’s always difficult to determine where to rank a guy fresh off the IL and he could move up The List quickly.

33. Logan Webb (SFG) – Webb is doing Webb things. It’s business per usual.

34. Shota Imanaga (CHC) –  Imanaga quelled our fears with 91/92 mph velocity and ten punchouts, even if it came with a frustrating three-run HR to ruin the ERA.

35. Bryan Woo (SEA) – Despite great results, I had no choice but to lower Woo with the report of shoulder soreness. I can’t do anything until I get more information and if it means he hits the IL, Kade Anderson is sure to appear this week.

36. George Kirby (SEA) – Kirby. Buddy. You were given the Astros, where you had all the freedom to execute the approach, and you couldn’t. WHAT DO WE DO WITH YOU. Maybe move you to third base side of the rubber…

37. Kevin Gausman (CHC) – I see Gausman struggle and I shrug. It’s too good of a scenario and he’s pitching incredibly close to what we’ve always seen. It’ll even out, don’t worry.

38. Jared Jones (PIT) – Jones had some rough patches against the Red Sox, but was able to wriggle out of jams. I still question if this is too high a rank above safer plays below, but then again, Jones has all the stuff to be a Top 20 arm.

 

 

Tier 6 – What Will They Become?

 

39. Dustin May (MIL) – May isn’t getting the whiffs we want to see from his sweeper (yet), and is still a reliable arm for a winning team.

40. Walbert Ureña (LAA) – Ureña rarely lets us down. He’s really doing José a service.

41. Peter Lambert (HOU) – It’s the tier of fantastic pickups and Lambert has produced so regularly for managers all year.

42. Ranger Suarez (BOS) – Suarez looked much more in rhythm last start (phew) and I’m back on this train.

43. Ian Seymour (TBR) – It’s not the prettiest stuff of the lot, but the changeup is still doing plenty of work and racking up strikeouts.

44. Jacob Lopez (ATH) – He’s doing what he did last summer. It’s AWESOME.

45. Noah Cameron (KCR) – Cameron just returned a one-hit CGSHO. Yes, that is unreal. The upped velocity and reduced fastball focus is doing wonders.

46. Joey Cantillo (CLE) – The velocity and curveball are there. It’s what you want.

47. Emerson Hancock (SEA) – Hancock executed the perfect RHB approach with sinkers in and sweepers away (are you watching Kirby?!) and I hope we get it every time.

48. Eduardo Rodriguez (ARI) – He’s on the Vargas Rule of his career. Let it ride.

49. Ryan Weathers (NYY) – It’s interesting seeing Weathers allow far fewer HRs and hold a great ERA at the cost of a high WHIP and fewer strikeouts. Which is better? A 4.36 ERA, 1.17 WHIP, 26% strikeout rate or a 2.70 ERA, 1.29 WHIP and 22% strikeout rate?

 

Tier 7 – This Should Be More

 

50. Kyle Bradish (BAL) – Bradish is simply not getting strikeouts with his slider and curveball. It’s maddening. I don’t quite understand why and it doesn’t add up.

51. Jackson Jobe (DET) – Jobe looked great, then looked blegh. The velocity was still there in his second start, though, with a flatter HAVAA than I receall before TJS. If he can get the heater upstairs, the rest should fall into place.

52. Michael King (SDP) – The strikes are returning for King and I’m encouraged he’ll settle in for the final weeks of the year.

53. Nick Lodolo (CIN) – We’ve seen two starts of Lodolo since returning from the IL and I was impressed by his changeup feel recently. Not the rest of his approach, sadly.

54. MacKenzie Gore (TEX) – Gore had himself a lovely time with his changeup, then went to Sacré Verde where he consistently spotted four-seamers upstairs atypical to his normal self. I sure hope that returns.

55. Hunter Brown (HOU) – What is up with Brown’s fastball? It was yet another game around a 50% strike rate with his four-seamer and I’m starting to lose hope that he’ll be that guy again.

 

Tier 8 –  You’re Generally Safe. Ish.

 

56. Sean Manaea (NYM) – Manaea’s last outing wasn’t as pretty as the others, but his four-seamer is still the pitch we want it to be.

57. Tyler Mahle (ATL) – Mahle is cooking right now. His command across his last few starts has been as good as anyone you’ll find in the majors.

58. Brandon Pfaadt (ARI) – This is a gorgeous Vargas Rule and you’re not stopping after he survived multiple strong offenses.

59. Shane McClanahan (TBR) – We saw fewer than 60 pitches in his Still ILL on Monday, while McShane hasn’t given us many fantastic outings across the last few months. He should still be helpful and up to 70+ pitches next time, but without great command, I’m curious how high the ceiling actually goes.

60. Christian Scott (NYM) – Scott is nailing the top of the zone effectively with four-seamers and cutters to LHB, and I sure hope he embraces the 90/91 mph “sinker” (I think it’s a changeup) to attack the bottom half of the zone consistently.

61. Brayan Bello (BOS) – Bello is a safe Win machine as long as he has an opener when he pitches. Limited upside, sadly, given his low volume.

62. Michael Soroka (ARI) – We’ve seen two starts from Soroka now and neither came with his sparkling pitch separation of the spring. I expect it to return in time, but don’t overvalue that potential.

63. Clay Holmes (CHC) – We just witnessed the perfect showcase of how The Adobe is a perfect fit for the Cubs’ defense.

 

Tier 9 – WHAT DO I DO WITH YOU

 

64. Joe Musgrove (SDP) – We haven’t seen Musgrove since 2023. His last rehab start gave us 95 mph heaters (not 91!), but deadzone four-seamer movement. The cutter had a wide movement band and should be the focus of his approach, but we should all temper our expectations and label his return a Still ILL and hope for the best. He’ll be limited to 75-80 pitches or so after tossing 70 here.

65. José Soriano (TOR) – I’m not sure what we’re going to get from Soriano. His four-seamer is effective when it gets strikes, and has for two straight, while his curveball disappeared just one start after running with it as his favorite pitch. It’s almost like he’s a volatile HIPSTER…

66. Bryce Miller (SEA) – Miller just threw more splitters than any other pitch. That’s right, more than his four-seamer. It doesn’t make a whole lot of sense and I’m as confused as ever. This is nothing like the dude flinging 97 mph heaters with stellar breakers before the all-star break.

67. Robbie Ray (SDP) – Give me one more start with Ray for the Padres. I’m so curious if he’s able to locate sliders to RHB again, paired with effective high heaters and low changeups.

68. Taj Bradley (MIN) – He’s the same chaotic man he’s always been.

69. Shane Bieber (TOR) – Bieber finally gave me the outing I’ve wanted with curveballs looking filthy once again. Please do that some more and retain the added four-seamer movement.

70. Emmet Sheehan (LAD) – He’s being called back up with Wrobleski’s injury and if you’re looking for a possible needle-pusher in the final stretch, Sheehan could be that guy who is hanging out on your wire right now.

71. Roki Sasaki (LAD) – It’s the same HIPSTER story with Sasaki. Good luck.

 

Tier 10 – It’s Something. It’s A Toby.

 

72. Casey Mize (SDP) – I was worried he was injured, but he sat 93/94 mph in Cleveland (sweet!), and that’s good enough for me. So wild to see a 0% CSW splitter return a high strike rate across 22 thrown.

73. Michael Wacha (KCR) – He’s such a Spider-Man.

74. Nick Martinez (TBR) – This is an incredible Vargas Rule with a sub-15% strikeout rate. I sure hope it lasts for the next six weeks.

75. Foster Griffin (CLE) – Griffin isn’t getting strikeouts and it feels as though the book is out on him. At the same time, he’s still a decent play for six frames of decent production.

76. Matthew Boyd (CHC) – Boyd got clobbered by Joshua “Dopest MLB Debut since Strasburg” Baez. He should be fine.

77. Shane Baz (BAL) – He ran into some bad luck last start and I hope his cutter returns to the fold.

 

Tier 11 – You Really Don’t Have To Do This

 

78. Landen Roupp (SFG) – We’re at the point that despite my belief that he’s better than the results have been, Roupp simply isn’t getting the results his pitch separation should produce. So we wait.

79. Bubba Chandler (PIT) – He seems so close, but cannot execute a gameplan at all. Maybe it’ll just click one day and POOF! There’s a stud.

80. Will Warren (NYY) – Warren is awfully disappointing, but still has a rotation spot with Fried’s injury, and there may be a few more cheap Wins here.

81. Robert Gasser (MIL) – I loved Gasser’s cutter last time out and I’m encouraged to give this more of a whirl with that pitch in his pocket.

82. Connor Prielipp (MIN) – That line from Prielipp was baaad. It doesn’t mean he’s that forever of course, but hot dang, I understand chasing something else. Don’t forget the fun strikeout marks before it.

83. Framber Valdez (DET) – Valdez pitched better than the line suggested last time out, with a more reliable curveball than most of his starts this year. I still cannot tell you he’ll be better in the next few weeks, but the talent is still in there.

84. Kyle Leahy (STL) – Leahy has gotten the results without enough development to justify it. By all means, Vargas Rule it, but this ranking should tell you that I don’t buy it.

85. Cristian Javier (HOU) – Javier’s cutter addition is a welcome sight and should propel far better results than the last two games we’ve seen.

86. George Klassen (LAA) – We saw Klassen feature a great strike rate in his final starts in the minors, plummet in his return to the majors, then pound the zone last time for a successful game. I’m inclined to believe that he’s shifted his approach to throw down the pipe and not the edges, which can make him a serviceable arm with a legit gyro slider and mid-to-upper 90s velocity.

87. Freddy Peralta (TBR) – Oh Peralta. I dug the high four-seamer during your first start in the Trop wearing the Rays uni, but everything else disappeared. I just want one start where at least two pitches are cooking. Just two.

 

Tier 12 – Arms To Consider This Week

 

88. Cody Bradford (TEX) – He’s fine. He had to deal with Sacré Verde and the matchups should be better, but the ceiling is limited given the 89/90 mph velocity n all.

89. Brandon Young (BAL) – Young didn’t excel as I expected in Tampa Bay (Only five splitters? Why?!) and it’s possible the secondaries are better in a repeat matchup in Camden with the four-seamer leading the way.

90. Dean Kremer (MIN) – His revenge game was dope. The follow-up wasn’t close, but he was awfully unlucky to allow a three-run HR off a high-and-tight splitter in his final frame. He may be still a worthy arm.

91. Anthony Kay (CHW) – You’re not going to get a ton of value from Kay, but there is something useful in here.

92. Keider Montero (DET) – Montero is a stellar WHIP play. Nothing more.

93. Martín Pérez (ATL) – Pérez had a terrible first inning, then settled down to grab four shutout frames. He’s still a good shot at a cheap Win with three decent matchups ahead.

94. Jake Bennett (BOS) – This seemed like a better version of Bennett, with a lovely four-seamer and changeup split and a 16% SwStr rate, but the fourth inning messed him up. At least he gets the Giants this week.

95. Gage Jump (ATH) – He’ll be in Kansas City and not Sacré Verde. It could be a strikeout explosion.

96. Hunter Dobbins (STL) – Dobbins brought the velocity back up to 94 mph, but the overall command has me weirded out a little too much to lean into him heavily.

97. Ethan Pecko (HOU) – Pecko is making his MLB debut and I’m not too convinced he’s worth your time. He’s a kitchen-sink RHP at 94 mph with some whiffability, and who knows, maybe the Astros figure something out.

98. Adrian Houser (SFG) – Houser did well against a RHB-heavy squad, but now he’ll get a slew of LHB and I’m worried his four-seamer/sinker combo won’t be enough.

99. Zac Thornton (NYM) – He’s been a great QS play, but for your standard leagues, he’s not striking enough batters out. No surprise there – it’s low-90s heaters with a cutter to jam RHB.

100. Will Dion (WSH) – This looks out of left field and I get it. I watched him against the Mets and I have a soft spot for the southpaw with 7.1 feet extension, an 89-90 mph heater, and an Estrada-esque changeup. I also hope he throws the 85/86 mph cutter a bit more to pair off the fastball. This could be a sneaky play with opener Brad Lord likely going a few frames again, setting Dion up for more potential Wins.

Kohl Drake (ARI) – There may be something here in time, but Drake needs more polish first.

Merrill Kelly (ARI) – He couldn’t even take down Rockie Road with a slew of LHB to serve his changeup. Yeesh.
